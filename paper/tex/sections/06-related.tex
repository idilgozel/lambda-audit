\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Memoryless policies and eligibility traces.} That the best
memoryless policy in a POMDP is stochastic and that TD-style learning of it
can fail without state estimation was established by
\citet{singh1994learning} and \citet{jaakkola1995reinforcement}, building on
the perceptual-aliasing problem of \citet{whitehead1991learning}. The closest
antecedent to our result is \citet{loch1998using}, who showed empirically that
Sarsa($\lambda$) with large $\lambda$ finds better memoryless policies than
$\lambda=0$ on aliased gridworlds. We give the mechanism an exact form: a
closed-form map from $\lambda$ to the equilibrium the averaged actor--critic
update converges to, a cost law with a predicted threshold, and a proposition
stating when the effect is absent.

\paragraph{Critic bias under partial observability.} The bias--variance
trade-off of TD($\lambda$) is classical \citep{kearns2000bias}; that an
observation-restricted critic biases the policy gradient in POMDPs, and that a
state-conditioned critic can remove the bias, is developed by
\citet{baisero2022unbiased}. Our contribution is orthogonal: we hold the critic
observation-restricted, as memory benchmarks do, and compute how far and in
which direction the bootstrap horizon moves the learned policy.

\paragraph{Hyperparameter studies.} Large-scale studies of on-policy
actor--critic methods find the advantage estimator's $\lambda$ among the
sensitive choices \citep{andrychowicz2021what}, and the reproducibility
literature documents how implementation and configuration decide outcomes
\citep{henderson2018deep,engstrom2020implementation}. Those studies are on
fully observed control; none isolates the interaction between $\lambda$ and
hidden-state timescale that a memory comparison manipulates.

\paragraph{Memory benchmarks.} POPGym \citep{morad2023popgym} and Memory Gym
\citep{pleines2023memory} rank architectures at a fixed $\lambda$; recurrent
baselines are tuned the same way \citep{hausknecht2015deep,ni2022recurrent}.
The scope proposition says which of these environments the confound can reach
at all, and \S\ref{sec:results} shows both outcomes under one harness.

\paragraph{Preregistration and statistical practice.} We follow the
interquartile-mean and bootstrap-interval conventions of
\citet{agarwal2021deep}, and take preregistration as method from
\citet{nosek2018preregistration}. What we add is the executable form:
constants and scorers frozen in code, checked mechanically at deployment, with
capability gates scored before predictions and every registered outcome
reported.
