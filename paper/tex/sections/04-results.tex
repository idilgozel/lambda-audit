\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We report every registered prediction. Of 39 scored, 18 passed and 21
failed, and one (P22.2) is unscored by design; capability gates are tallied
separately (22 passed, 2 failed)
because a failed gate means the predictions behind it are \emph{not
scored} rather than refuted. The ledger is Table~\ref{tab:ledger}. The failures are not
incidental to the results --- three of them are the mechanism by which
the paper's scope claim is tested, and the one that cost the most
produced the paper's strongest prediction.

\subsection{The confound, made
exact}

On the reference instance the aliased equilibrium map runs from
\(k_{\text{AC}} = 24.79\) at \(\lambda = 0\) to \(1.51\) at
\(\lambda = 1\), against a best memoryless gain of
\(k_{\text{ml}} = 1.649\): the learning target of the memoryless
baseline moves by an order of magnitude under a hyperparameter that is
rarely reported to two decimals. The map is a closed-form root of the
exact expected GAE(\(\lambda\)) gradient, not a fit.

The learned agent's cost follows. On the coupled arm, cost at
\(\lambda = 0.9\) exceeds cost at \(\lambda = 1.0\) by \(2.63\times\)
(P6.2, registered threshold \(1.25\times\)), and at \(\lambda = 1.0\)
the learned gain lands at \(1.663\) against \(k_{\text{ml}} = 1.649\)
--- the memoryless baseline is only the \emph{best} memoryless policy at
the top of the \(\lambda\) range.

One registered prediction about \emph{location} failed here and reshaped
the rest of the paper. P6.1 required the learned gain to land at
\(k_{\text{AC}}(\lambda)\) below the horizon; at
\(\lambda \in \{0.8, 0.95\}\) it landed nearer \(k_{\text{ml}}\), and
cost missed its 15\% band by 96--425\%. Training does not \emph{arrive}
at the computed fixed point below the horizon. That failure separated
two questions the design had conflated --- where the equilibrium is, and
whether training reaches it --- and forced the second threshold (\S3.4)
into the theory.

\subsection{The cost law, tested
prospectively}

E7 tested the quantitative law by locating \(\lambda_{1/2}\) from
trained agents across four instances. All three predictions failed:
\(\lambda_{1/2}\) within 0.01 on 1 of 4 instances (P7.1), the ordering
in \(\gamma h\) broken (P7.2), the below-knee plateau within 10\% on 1
of 4 (P7.3).

The diagnosis, performed on the landed data and labelled post-hoc: the
registered estimator conflated the cost law \(J(k)\) with the assumption
that training arrives at \(k_{\text{AC}}\). Every below-knee seed stalls
at 6--50\% of its target, and the estimator anchored on cells
contaminated by off-manifold excursions. Decomposed, the law itself was
untouched --- across the 23 excursion-free cells, measured cost matched
the analytic cost \emph{of the gain each run actually reached} with
median ratio 0.994, 22 of 23 within 10\%.

E10 tested this prospectively rather than arguing it. Placing a
one-parameter policy \textbf{at} \(k_{\text{AC}}(\lambda)\) by
construction removes the reachability failure by design, and the
registered prediction is the same quantity E7 got wrong. P11.3: measured
\(\lambda_{1/2}\) falls within 0.01 of its grid-matched analytic target
on \textbf{all four} instances, with absolute differences of 0.00021,
0.00014, 0.00101 and 0.00497 --- a frozen point prediction hit at four
locations, at one to two orders of magnitude inside its own tolerance.
This is the paper's central empirical result.

\subsection{The scope law, confirmed in both
directions}

The scope claim is that the confound requires actions to influence the
hidden state: when transitions are action-independent, the critic's bias
cancels identically from every action preference at every \(\lambda\)
(\S3.2). Because it predicts \emph{absence}, it is testable in a way most
mechanism claims are not.

\textbf{In-domain.} On the decoupled arm the largest learned gain across
the whole \(\lambda\) grid is 0.0079 with spread 0.0091 (P6.3), against
a coupled-arm difference of 5.853 at \(\lambda = 0.9\) --- an arm ratio
of 3.52 (P6.4). Same representation, same optimiser, same evaluator;
only the coupling differs.

\textbf{External, scope condition met.} On position-only CartPole, where
the agent applies force and the hidden velocity responds, all three
registered predictions passed: the memoryless architecture steps down
below the horizon (Easy \(-0.0213\), 95\% CI \([-0.0288, -0.0140]\);
Hard \(-0.0060\), CI \([-0.0095, -0.0018]\)), no step is resolvable
above it (six comparisons, every CI containing zero), and the memory
architecture's across-seed variance concentrates at low \(\lambda\) by a
pooled factor of 42.6 (P9.1--P9.3).

\textbf{External, scope condition not met.} Two POPGym game environments
were tested and neither shows the effect. On BattleshipEasy the capability
gates failed (both arms at the floor), so P12.1--P12.3 are \emph{not scored}.
On MineSweeperMedium the gates passed --- our memoryless arm reproduces POPGym's
published MLP score to $0.012$, and the memory advantage at $\lambda=1.0$ is
$+0.121$ --- and all three registered predictions then failed: the advantage
at $\lambda=0.95$ is $+0.091$, \emph{smaller} than at $1.0$, the opposite of
the registered direction (P15.1), and no structure appears at the computed
knee (P15.3). We registered these as positive predictions and report them as
failures. The reading the scope law supplies: in both games the latent is
fixed at reset and actions \emph{reveal} it rather than steer it, so there is
no equilibrium to displace; that distinction was not in our claim set when
E12 was designed, and the $\tau$ used to place its knee was an episode length
rather than a relaxation time. Both errors are ours. Independently, the
released diagnostic (\S\ref{sec:diagnostic}) finds no trace of the mechanism's
signature in E12's training curves --- memoryless-arm excursions are at noise
level and flat across $\lambda$ rather than concentrated below the knee. The
instrument reports the effect absent, not swamped (Appendix~\ref{app:e12}).

\textbf{Second in-scope external (E14).} On position-only Pendulum, where
torque steers a hidden angular velocity, all six per-variant capability gates
passed (Easy: mlp 0.3663, gru 0.9018, gap $+0.5355$; Hard: mlp 0.3615, gru
0.7907, gap $+0.4292$) and two of the three registered predictions passed. The
memoryless architecture's score steps down from above the knee bracket
$[0.948, 0.979]$ to below it (Easy $-0.0245$, 95\% CI $[-0.0290, -0.0137]$;
Hard $-0.0066$, CI $[-0.0106, -0.0014]$; P17.1), and above the bracket it is
equivalent to $\lambda=1$ within the registered $\pm0.05$ (pooled $-0.0026$,
CI $[-0.0096, +0.0123]$, and $-0.0022$, CI $[-0.0072, +0.0022]$; largest
single-cell deviations 0.0064 and 0.0048; P17.2). The memory architecture's
across-seed variance is ordered low-$\lambda$ over high-$\lambda$ on Easy
(ratio 23.41) but not on Hard (ratio 0.06); the pooled ratio 2.17 clears the
registered 2.0 and the registered ordering in every variant does not hold, so
P17.3 fails. Frozen scorer, verbatim: \texttt{VERDICTS: \{'G14a-Easy': 'PASS', 'G14b-Easy': 'PASS', 'G14c-Easy': 'PASS', 'G14a-Hard': 'PASS', 'G14b-Hard': 'PASS', 'G14c-Hard': 'PASS', 'P17.1': 'PASS', 'P17.2': 'PASS', 'P17.3': 'FAIL'\}}.

\subsection{Generality beyond the minimal instance}

\textbf{Kernel and dimension.} Recomputing the exact objects of
\S\ref{sec:theory} for Prony kernels at state dimensions 2--4, including
non-Markovian observed dynamics, reproduces the same structure at every
kernel: an inflated equilibrium below a knee, a 14--16$\times$ collapse
across the grid, and both thresholds. Two properties are \emph{not}
kernel-universal and are reported: $\lambda_{1/2}$ is not ordered by kernel
correlation time across kernel \emph{shapes} (it is within the single-mode
family, P10.2), and $\lambda^\ast<\lambda_{1/2}$ inverts for a slow minority
mode.

\textbf{Nonlinearity.} E13 added a cubic damping the theory cannot absorb,
sized at 10\% and 50\% of the dominant balance at the above-knee equilibrium,
and asked whether the \emph{linearized} theory still locates the threshold.
It does: measured $\lambda_{1/2}$ is $0.985529$ and $0.985542$ against
$0.983837$ --- within $0.0017$ at a registered tolerance of $0.005$ --- while
the perturbation moves the floor cost by $2.5\times$ and sharpens the collapse
(profile ratio $1.34\to1.82\to3.38$). The zero-perturbation control
reproduces the linearized value to $1.8\times10^{-5}$ (G13, P16.1, P16.2).

\textbf{Drive distribution.} At matched autocovariance the equilibrium
\emph{location} moves $3.4\times$ across Gaussian, bounded and kurtotic
drives while the cost at equilibrium stays within 1--7\% of $r\Theta K(0)$
(companion derivation). This split predicts the ledger: every failed
location prediction (P6.1, P11.1, P10.1) is in the fragile class; every
passed cost or threshold prediction (P6.2, P11.3, P16.1--.2, and the cost
map holding to $\le1.05\%$ at every measured gain in all 32 E10 cells) is in
the robust class.

\subsection{The mechanism: what is confirmed, and what is
not}

The off-manifold stability index \(S(\lambda, k)\) carries the bootstrap
weight \((1-\lambda)/\lambda\) as its destabilising factor and predicts
a second threshold. It is validated against Monte Carlo to 1--3\% at ten
points. Its experimental record is mixed, and the preregistration fixed
in advance how to read that.

What passed: the predicted onset falls with \(\gamma h\) in the measured
ordering (P10.2); and in the perturbation-decay test, all 8 cells
predicted unstable read unstable (P11.4's unstable side, 8/8).

What failed: the onset bracket contains \(\lambda^\ast\) on 2 of 3
prospective instances, and the miss sits \emph{below} the predicted
bracket, which the frozen file classifies as a hard failure rather than
an incomplete mechanism (P10.1). The predicted widening of the gap
between the two thresholds does not appear --- measured gaps are 0.0066,
0.0054 and 0.0080 where the theory predicts 0.0010, 0.0095 and 0.0570
(P10.3). And 18 of 21 cells predicted stable read stable, against a
registered allowance of 19 (P11.4).

\texttt{e7-diagnosis-cost-law.md} \S5, written before E10 ran, states
that P11.4 failing beyond its allowance demotes the bias-direction
mechanism ``to a suggestive calculation''. We apply that rule. The
mechanism is reported as suggestive, not confirmed.

The three P11.4 misses are the three scored cells with the smallest positive
$S$, each adjacent to its instance's knee, and resolvability at this budget is
asymmetric (growth resolves to $|S|=1.3\times10^{-4}$, decay only to
$S\ge1.1\times10^{-2}$); the registered floor was calibrated on a growth cell
and applied symmetrically, a design error we would not repeat. Neither
observation is offered as a rescue (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism}). P11.1 and
P11.2 also failed --- 19 of 32 cells drifted outside the 15\% gain tolerance
--- and their verified decomposition is that all 96 runs drifted
\emph{downward}, the analytic cost map holds to $\le1.05\%$ at every measured
gain including every failing cell, and failure aligns with the branch of the
equilibrium map rather than with $\lambda$ (Appendix~\ref{app:drift}). P11.3
is estimated from the shape of that intact cost profile, which is why it
survives what P11.1 and P11.2 did not.

\subsection{The diagnostic}
\label{sec:diagnostic}

The intermittency statistic is released as a tool practitioners can run
on any continuous-control log without adopting our theory. Its three
registered tripwires fired zero false positives (0 of 40 decoupled-arm
runs, 0 of 40 on the no-onset instance, 0 of 180 above-horizon
memoryless runs), and its one prospective specificity prediction passed
on 50 runs with zero flags (P13.1). Sensitivity against a privileged
detector is 17 of 23; the tool is deliberately blind to chronic
elevation, which is documented as a scope property rather than tuned
away.
